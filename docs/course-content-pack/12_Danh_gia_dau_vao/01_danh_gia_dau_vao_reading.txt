[TITLE] Interpreting a survey
[CONTENT]
A university asked students whether recorded lectures helped them study. Most respondents described the recordings as useful, especially when reviewing an unfamiliar explanation. A report then claimed that recordings had improved examination performance. That conclusion goes beyond the survey: students reported perceived usefulness, while examination outcomes were not measured. Moreover, participation was voluntary, so respondents may not represent all students. These limitations do not show that recordings are ineffective. They show that evidence for one claim cannot automatically establish another. A stronger evaluation would examine how recordings are used, compare relevant learning outcomes and consider differences in prior knowledge. Student opinions remain valuable for understanding experience, but should not be presented as a direct measurement of achievement.
