Samantha: Speaker one, Lena. Before joining the mentoring programme, I had a rather vague idea of what a project coordinator did. My mentor described the daily responsibilities, including the less exciting administrative work. That helped me identify a realistic next step instead of applying for every job with an attractive title. I appreciated that she did not promise instant promotion. We discussed what I could practise in my current role. The programme was most useful when it turned a general ambition into a specific action.

Daniel: Speaker two, Omar. The mentor was helpful, but the organisation of the sessions made participation difficult. Several meetings coincided with my work shift, so attending required a change that my manager could not always approve. I would offer the same session at different times. I also think a single final meeting is less useful than brief follow-up conversations. Advice that sounds clear on the day can become confusing when you try to apply it. A short conversation two weeks later could help resolve those questions.

Karen: Speaker three, Priya. I expected most of the learning to come from the mentor, but I learned a great deal from other participants. Hearing someone explain what had gone wrong in an interview helped me notice a similar weakness in my own preparation. Still, I would not simply copy that person's career plan. People have different responsibilities and opportunities. The useful lesson is to adapt advice to your own circumstances. The programme gave us examples to think with, not a single route everyone must follow.
